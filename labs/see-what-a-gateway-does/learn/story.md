minutes: 2

Welcome to the platform team at Larkfield. First, the museum tour of how we used to call models.

Exhibit one: four product teams, four providers, four keys, each in its own repository. One key also lived in a screenshot in a team chat. Everybody liked the screenshot. Nobody noticed the key.

Exhibit two: a Saturday, three in the morning. Support's provider went down and Priya's assistant stopped answering customers. Search's assistant carried on happily, and we could not point Support at it, because the provider's name was written into its code.

Exhibit three: a provider renamed one of its models. Three of our apps had the old name typed in by hand. All three broke before breakfast.

Exhibit four: Jonas in Finance gets one invoice a month and asks which team spent it and which provider answered. We give him a shrug. I have got very good at the shrug.

Exhibit five, my favourite: a research script retried one failing call from Friday evening to Monday morning. It spent the month's budget and was still asking, very politely.

So Tomasz built a gateway: one front door for every model call. A team asks for a plain name such as support, never a provider. The gateway holds the keys, picks where the call goes and writes down every call it handles.

Three more teams want to move in next month. Before they do, I want someone who has never seen it to tell us what it really does, not what Tomasz's notes say it does. His notes are mostly right. Mostly.

That someone is you. We have built you a small copy with a pretend provider, so no real bill. Send it a call, then find out where the call went, what was counted, and how the gateway complains when it cannot help.
