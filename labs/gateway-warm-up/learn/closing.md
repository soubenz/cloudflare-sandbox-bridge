Three months later, on another Saturday at three in the morning, OpenAI went down again, but this time Tomas's phone stayed dark and he slept right through it.

What had changed was that Larkfield's apps no longer called providers directly; they all called one door, a gateway, which checks who is asking and holds the keys for every provider, so the apps never have to. Ollie: "So I can't paste a key I never had. Got it."

Follow the support assistant's call through that night: it asks for support rather than for a model, because support is an alias, Larkfield's own name for a job, and the gateway looks it up in its table.

The table lists OpenAI first and Anthropic second, so when OpenAI fails to answer, the gateway quietly sends the same call on to Anthropic, using a key only the gateway holds, and the customer still gets a reply.

As Anthropic's answer goes back, the gateway writes one line in its log with the team, the alias and the tokens used, one thousand eight hundred and fifty of them, a token being a small piece of text that providers bill by.

At the end of the month Graham opens that log, and because every row carries a team's name, the single number on the invoice finally breaks down, team by team. Graham: "Every line has a name. I could get used to this."

And when the next new model comes out, the platform team changes one line in that table, so every app that asks for support moves to the new model without a single change of its own. Jess: "So I can try the new model today? Today today?"

Next come two short games about what you have just seen, and after them the first lab, where you send calls to a real gateway and watch what it does with them.
